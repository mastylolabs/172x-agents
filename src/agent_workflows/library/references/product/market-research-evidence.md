# Market research evidence

Use this reference when an approved discovery question requires current external evidence about
users, alternatives, positioning, regulation, pricing, distribution, or market constraints. It is
not permission to browse broadly, fabricate primary research, forecast without a method, or make
the product decision.

## Required inputs and readiness

- Identified discovery brief and explicit research questions.
- Target user or buyer, geography, segment, use case, and decision to be informed.
- Consistent comparison dimensions and material constraints.
- Browsing, source, time, access, and citation limits.
- Known sources, terminology, relevant date boundary, and decision owner.

An undefined target or question blocks a universal conclusion. Unavailable browsing, paywalled
material, stale evidence, or missing primary sources limits findings rather than authorizing
invention. If the research would collect personal or sensitive data or perform an external action,
obtain the required authority first.

## Staged method

1. **Frame each question.** State the decision it informs, target context, terms, date boundary,
   acceptable evidence, and what is explicitly outside the research.
2. **Design the source plan.** Identify likely primary sources, official publications, direct
   product documentation, public records, and only then useful secondary synthesis.
3. **Retrieve consistently.** Record publisher, title, publication or update date, retrieval date,
   geography, segment, version where relevant, and accessible location. Do not cite a search result
   page as the source.
4. **Extract claim-level evidence.** Capture only the proposition the source supports. Separate
   direct statements, observed product behavior, reported research, and publisher interpretation.
5. **Assess applicability.** Evaluate authority, incentive, methodology, freshness, geography,
   population, definitions, and whether the source actually answers the question.
6. **Compare alternatives fairly.** Use the same approved dimensions and evidence date boundary.
   Mark unavailable or incomparable cells rather than treating absence as a negative feature.
7. **Reconcile contradictions.** Preserve both claims, explain date/definition/context differences,
   and mark the conclusion conditional or unknown when evidence cannot discriminate.
8. **Synthesize cautiously.** Separate sourced facts, supported inference, implications, assumptions,
   unknowns, and decisions. Complete `references/common/handoff-envelope.md` for the human gate.

## Source selection rules

| Claim type | Prefer | Common limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product capability or support | Current official documentation for the named version | Marketing pages may omit conditions or lifecycle state |
| Price or commercial term | Dated official pricing or supplied contract | Geography, tier, usage, tax, and change over time |
| Regulation or public standard | Issuing authority or versioned primary publication | Applicability still requires authorized interpretation |
| Market size or adoption | Transparent primary dataset or research methodology | Definitions, sample, incentives, and forecasting uncertainty |
| User need | Supplied direct research artifacts with provenance | Public commentary is not representative research |
| Alternative comparison | Equivalent primary sources for every alternative | Missing data is unknown, not zero or absent |

Use secondary sources to discover or contextualize primary evidence, not to launder an unsupported
claim. Record versions or retrieval dates when external facts may change materially.

## Normal and failure paths

On the normal path, each question has a bounded answer or explicit unknown, every external claim
has provenance and applicability, alternatives share comparison dimensions, contradictions remain
visible, and the human receives decision implications without a go/no-go claim.

If no adequate source exists, report the evidence gap and smallest next research step. If sources
conflict, do not average incompatible claims. If browsing is unavailable, work only from supplied
materials and state the limit. If the target geography, segment, or date changes, invalidate
affected conclusions. If research exposes a product or technical question, route it instead of
answering outside market authority.

## Common mistakes

- Browsing broadly before defining the decision question.
- Citing vendor promotion as independent adoption evidence.
- Combining sources from different dates, geographies, populations, or definitions silently.
- Ranking alternatives with inconsistent or undisclosed criteria.
- Treating a missing feature mention as proof the feature is absent.
- Inventing interviews, quotes, market size, forecast, confidence, or source access.
- Reproducing vendor documentation instead of recording decision-relevant evidence.
- Converting research implications into product approval or implementation requirements.

## Calibration

**Good:** “For US healthcare teams as of the recorded retrieval dates, official documentation for
A and B confirms audit export; C's public documentation does not establish it, so C is unverified,
not absent. The older European comparison uses a different residency definition and does not resolve
the US question. Human decision remains open.”

**Counterexample:** “A is the market leader and best choice because its website says most
enterprises trust it.” The source, population, definition, date, comparison, and decision authority
are unsupported.

## Evidence expectations

Cite every externally verifiable claim to the direct source with author or publisher, date or
retrieval date, context, and relevant version when material. Link each inference to claim-level
evidence and describe its applicability limits. State sources not accessed, search boundaries,
contradictions, and unknown comparison cells. Do not imply current facts without current evidence.

## Escalation triggers

Escalate undefined target market or comparison criteria; conflicting primary evidence that changes
the decision; unavailable material sources; regulated or policy interpretation; research requiring
personal data, credentials, spend, or external contact; unsupported numeric forecast; or a request
to approve product direction, select architecture, or claim research not performed.
